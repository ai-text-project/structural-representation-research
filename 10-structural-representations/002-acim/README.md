# Purpose

This directory contains the Structural Representation of ACIM as a structured conceptual system.

The purpose of this case is not to summarize ACIM, reproduce its source materials, or provide a general doctrinal explanation.

Instead, the case externalizes the internal organization of the research object through a sequence of differentiated structural representations.

The resulting assets provide observation surfaces for examining:

- structural organization,
- conceptual layers,
- functional differentiation,
- dependency relationships,
- architectural relationships,
- concept-level connections,
- structural topology,
- and the resulting Canonical Structural Representation.

The governing principle is:

> **Represent the structure before comparing the object.**

# Formation Characteristic of the ACIM Case

The ACIM case has an important formation characteristic.

Its structural representation did not begin by assuming that the research object could be immediately reduced to a single architecture diagram.

Instead, structural organization became progressively visible through several distinct representational stages.

A useful high-level interpretation is:

```text
Framework Layer
      ↓
Functional Differentiation
      ↓
Architecture Relationships
      ↓
Structural Topology
      ↓
Canonical Structural Representation
```

This developmental sequence is significant because it preserves the difference between:

```text
identifying components
        ≠
understanding relationships
        ≠
observing topology
        ≠
stabilizing a canonical representation
```

The ACIM case should therefore be understood as a progressively formed Structural Representation rather than as a single diagrammatic model.
