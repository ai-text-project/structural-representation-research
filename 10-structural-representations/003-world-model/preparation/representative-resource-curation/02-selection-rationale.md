# Purpose

This document records the rationale for selecting the representative resources used in the World Model Structural Representation mission.

The frozen Representative Resource Set consists of:

1. **Primary Representative Resource**  
   Ding et al., *Understanding World or Predicting Future? A Comprehensive Survey of World Models*

2. **Complementary Representative Resource**  
   Demis Hassabis, Cambridge Lecture Transcript

The purpose of this document is to explain why these two resources form an appropriate observational input set for subsequent Structural Representation.

This document does not perform Structural Representation.

It does not determine a canonical World Model architecture.

---

# Selection Objective

The objective of Representative Resource Curation is not to collect the largest possible number of World Model publications.

The objective is to identify a minimal set of resources that:

- represents the target domain broadly;
- provides sufficient conceptual visibility;
- supports structural observation;
- remains publicly traceable;
- avoids unnecessary implementation detail;
- provides complementary rather than redundant perspectives;
- remains manageable as an observational input;
- supports subsequent Canonical Structural Representation.

The selection principle is therefore:

```text
Broad Resource Discovery
        ↓
Candidate Evaluation
        ↓
Representative Coverage
        ↓
Perspective Complementarity
        ↓
Minimal Resource Set
        ↓
Structural Representation Input
```

# Rationale for Primary Resource Selection

## Resource

**Ding et al. — _Understanding World or Predicting Future? A Comprehensive Survey of World Models_**

## Selection Role

**Primary Representative Resource**

## Primary Selection Rationale

This resource was selected as the Primary Representative Resource because it provides broad academic coverage of World Models as a research domain rather than focusing exclusively on one implementation, model family, or application.

Several characteristics make it particularly suitable for the present mission.

---

## 1. Domain-Level Orientation

The resource surveys World Models as a research field.

This is important because the target of the present mission is not one specific World Model implementation.

The target is the broader conceptual domain:

**World Model**

The resource therefore provides an appropriate starting point for identifying recurring concepts, functions, and research orientations across heterogeneous World Model approaches.

---

## 2. Broad Conceptual Coverage

The Survey organizes World Model research around broad functions including:

- understanding the world;
- internal representation;
- modeling world mechanisms;
- predicting future states;
- simulation;
- decision-making.

This provides substantial conceptual coverage for subsequent structural observation.

The Survey also extends across multiple application contexts rather than restricting the discussion to one implementation domain.

This breadth makes it useful as the principal academic observational input.

---

## 3. Explicit Organizational Structure

The Survey provides an explicit high-level distinction between:

```text
Understanding the World

and

Predicting the Future
```

This organizational structure is useful for Representative Resource Curation because it demonstrates that the resource contains sufficient conceptual organization for subsequent structural observation.

However:

```text
Survey Organization
        ≠
Canonical World Model Architecture
```

The Survey's taxonomy must not be automatically adopted as the resulting Structural Representation.

Its function is to provide evidence for observation, not to predetermine the output.

---

## 4. Cross-Application Coverage

World Model research appears across multiple areas of artificial intelligence.

A representative resource restricted to only one application domain could create an overly narrow observational basis.

The Survey provides broader coverage across different World Model research contexts.

This reduces the risk that subsequent Structural Representation will accidentally represent:

```text
One Application of World Models
```

as if it represented:

```text
The World Model Domain
```

---

## 5. Relative Recency

The Survey represents a relatively recent synthesis of the World Model research landscape.

This is important because World Model research is developing rapidly across areas such as:

- generative models;
- embodied systems;
- robotics;
- simulation;
- planning;
- predictive modeling;
- general-purpose AI systems.

A recent Survey provides a more suitable field-level observational baseline than relying exclusively on an earlier foundational implementation.

---

## 6. Public Accessibility

The resource is publicly accessible.

This supports:

- independent inspection;
- methodological transparency;
- provenance tracking;
- reproducibility;
- later validation.

Public traceability is an important requirement for Representative Resource Curation.

---

## 7. Appropriate Abstraction Level

The Survey provides more structural and conceptual information than a short introductory description while avoiding dependence on one implementation's low-level technical details.

This makes it appropriate for repository-level structural observation.

The Primary Resource therefore occupies a useful position between:

```text
Too General
        ↓
Insufficient Structural Visibility

        X

Comprehensive Survey
        ↓
Domain-Level Structural Visibility

        X

Too Implementation-Specific
        ↓
Architecture-Specific Detail
```

# Rationale for Complementary Resource Selection

## Resource

**Demis Hassabis — Cambridge Lecture Transcript**

## Selection Role

**Complementary Representative Resource**

## Complementary Selection Rationale

The Hassabis lecture was selected because it provides a perspective not supplied by the academic Survey alone.

Its primary value is not exhaustive World Model coverage.

Its value is the broader conceptual positioning of World Models within a trajectory toward increasingly general artificial intelligence.

---

## 1. General Intelligence Context

The lecture discusses World Models in relation to a broader AI development trajectory.

Relevant themes include:

- world understanding;
- learning;
- prediction;
- simulation;
- search;
- planning;
- interaction;
- action;
- general intelligence.

This provides a perspective beyond a field taxonomy.

---

## 2. World Understanding Perspective

The lecture associates World Models with increasingly general understanding of the world.

This makes the resource useful for observing the conceptual role assigned to World Models within a broader AI-system perspective.

The lecture therefore provides contextual information about:

```text
AI System
        ↓
World Understanding
        ↓
World Model
```

without requiring that this sequence be treated as a canonical architecture.

---

## 3. Search and Planning Context

A particularly important reason for retaining the lecture is its discussion of general models together with search and planning systems.

The lecture provides a conceptual trajectory involving:

```text
General Model / World Model
        ↓
Search
        ↓
Planning
        ↓
Real-World Achievement / Action
```

This perspective is useful because it situates World Models relative to capabilities that extend beyond representation or prediction alone.

Again, this relationship is observational input.

It is not adopted here as the canonical World Model structure.

---

## 4. Interaction and Physical-World Context

The lecture also discusses World Models in connection with:

- the real world;
- interaction;
- physical dynamics;
- simulation;
- generated environments.

This broadens the observational basis beyond purely abstract representation.

It may later help the Structural Representation Methodologist distinguish among:

```text
Representation

Prediction

Simulation

Interaction

Planning

Action
```

if such distinctions are supported by the full evidence.

The Representative Resource Curator does not determine those structural relationships.

---

## 5. Research-Trajectory Perspective

The lecture provides a developmental perspective extending across multiple areas of Hassabis's research trajectory.

The transcript discusses connections among topics such as:

- neuroscience;
- memory;
- imagination;
- planning;
- reinforcement learning;
- games;
- scientific discovery;
- World Models;
- AGI.

This does not make the lecture a comprehensive academic survey.

Instead, it provides a complementary conceptual trajectory through which the role of World Models can be observed.

---

# Why Two Different Resource Types Were Selected

The difference in resource type is intentional.

The resource set does not consist of:

```text
Survey A
        +
Survey B
```

because two similar surveys could increase source quantity without necessarily increasing observational diversity.

Instead, the selected configuration is:

```text
Academic Survey
        +
Research Lecture
```

Each performs a different methodological function.

# Why the Hassabis Lecture Is Not the Primary Resource

The Hassabis lecture contains valuable conceptual material.

However, it was not selected as the Primary Representative Resource because it does not attempt to systematically survey the complete World Model research field.

Its purpose is broader and more narrative.

Therefore:

```text
Hassabis Lecture
        ↓
Strong Conceptual Context
        ↓
Complementary

not

Comprehensive Domain Survey
        ↓
Primary
```

This distinction protects the mission from over-representing one researcher's conceptual framing as if it were the entire World Model research domain.

---

# Why the Survey Alone Is Not Sufficiently Rich for This Mission

The Ding Survey could potentially serve as a sufficient standalone resource for initial Structural Representation.

However, the present mission retains the Hassabis lecture because the lecture exposes a broader functional context involving:

- general world understanding;
- search;
- planning;
- interaction;
- real-world action;
- AGI.

These concepts are especially relevant when observing how World Models may function as part of larger intelligent systems.

The Complementary Resource therefore increases conceptual visibility without requiring a large expansion of the source corpus.

---

# Why Foundational World Models Papers Were Not Selected as Primary

Historically important research such as:

**Ha & Schmidhuber — _World Models_ (2018)**

remains highly relevant to the development of the field.

However, the present mission does not prioritize historical importance alone.

The objective is:

```text
Representative Domain Coverage
```

rather than:

```text
Historical Priority
```

A foundational paper may provide a particularly influential World Model architecture.

However:

```text
Influential Architecture
        ≠
Entire World Model Domain
```

Therefore, foundational papers remain available as supplementary or historical resources rather than being included automatically in the frozen Representative Resource Set.

---

# Why Additional Resources Were Not Selected

World Model research contains extensive literature concerning:

- model-based reinforcement learning;
- robotics;
- autonomous driving;
- video generation;
- embodied agents;
- predictive representation;
- latent-state models;
- generative simulation;
- cognitive models;
- planning systems.

Many of these resources are scientifically valuable.

However, Representative Resource Curation is not intended to become an exhaustive literature review.

The present mission therefore follows:

```text
Sufficient Domain Coverage
        +
Perspective Complementarity
        +
Source Traceability
        +
Manageable Input Size
        >
Maximum Source Quantity
```

Additional resources should be introduced only when a specific downstream observational problem requires them.

# Selection Limitations

The selected resource set has several deliberate limitations.

---

## Limitation 1 — World Model Is Not a Single Fixed Architecture

Unlike a specific cognitive architecture, World Model refers to a broad and heterogeneous research domain.

The downstream Methodologist should therefore not assume that all resources describe one identical architecture.

---

## Limitation 2 — Survey Taxonomy Is Not Universal Architecture

The organizational categories used by the Survey are analytically useful.

However, they should not automatically become the Structural Representation.

```text
Taxonomy
        ≠
Architecture
```

---

## Limitation 3 — Hassabis Perspective Is One Conceptual Perspective

The Hassabis lecture provides an important and authoritative perspective within contemporary AI research.

However, it remains one researcher's broader conceptual framing.

It should not automatically be treated as the universal definition of World Models.

---

## Limitation 4 — Historical Literature Is Not Exhaustively Represented

The frozen resource set does not reconstruct the historical development of World Model research.

Historical sources may be consulted later when necessary.

---

## Limitation 5 — Implementation Diversity Is Intentionally Compressed

Different World Model systems may use substantially different technical architectures.

The present resource-selection stage prioritizes higher-level conceptual coverage.

Implementation-specific diversity should be introduced only when required by subsequent Structural Representation.

---

# Comparison Bias Assessment

The World Model Structural Representation may later participate in Comparative Observation involving other representations.

Potential future comparison targets may include:

- SOAR;
- ACT-R;
- Transformer architectures;
- Knowledge Graph systems;
- AI instance internal structures;
- other cognitive or computational architectures.

However, the selected World Model resources were not chosen because they resemble those targets.

The selection criteria were internal to the World Model domain:

```text
Representativeness
        +
Conceptual Coverage
        +
Structural Observability
        +
Complementarity
        +
Traceability
```

Therefore:

**Comparison Neutrality: PASSED**

# Protection Against Structural Correspondence Bias

No attempt should be made at this stage to identify correspondences such as:

```text
World Model Component A
        ↔
SOAR Component B
```

or:

```text
World Model Function X
        ↔
AI Internal Structure Y
```

The correct sequence remains:

```text
World Model Resources
        ↓
World Model Structural Representation
        ↓
Canonical Structural Representation
        ↓
STABILIZATION / FREEZE
        ↓
══════════════════════════════════
      COMPARISON BOUNDARY
══════════════════════════════════
        ↓
Comparative Observation
        ↓
Structural Correspondence
```

This separation protects the World Model representation from being shaped by anticipated comparisons.

---

# Structural Neutrality Principle

The Representative Resource Curator does not determine in advance:

- what the major World Model layers are;
- whether World Models have universal modules;
- whether representation precedes prediction;
- whether prediction necessarily supports planning;
- whether search belongs inside or outside a World Model;
- whether planning belongs inside or outside a World Model;
- whether action is part of the World Model or part of a larger agent architecture;
- whether one canonical World Model architecture exists.

These are questions for downstream Structural Representation.

The selected resources provide observational evidence from which such questions may be investigated.
