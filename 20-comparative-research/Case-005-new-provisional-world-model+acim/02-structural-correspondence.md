# 1. Structural Units

## Projection A — World Model

The stable represented core consists of:

- World
- Representation
- Understanding
- Prediction

Possible extensions include:

- Search
- Planning
- Decision-Making
- Action

These possible extensions are not stabilized as members of the represented core.

Projection A therefore distinguishes between:

```text
Stable Structural Core
        ≠
Open / Unresolved Architectural Extension
```

---

## Projection B — ACIM

Projection B contains multiple differentiated structural granularities, including:

- Layers
- Functional Modules
- Concept Nodes
- Dependencies
- Architectural Responsibilities
- Architectural Coordinates
- Structural Relations

The represented object is therefore not organized through a single class of structural unit.

The following distinctions are preserved:

```text
Layer ≠ Module
Module ≠ Concept Node
Concept Node ≠ Architectural Coordinate
```

---

## Direct Structural Observation

Both representations contain identifiable structural units.

However, the existence of identifiable units is not by itself strong evidence of structural correspondence.

The more material observation is the difference in unit organization.

Projection A is comparatively concentrated around a small stable core.

Projection B is explicitly multi-granular.

Therefore:

> **Unit existence provides weak formal correspondence, while unit granularity and unit responsibility show material structural difference.**

### Restabilized Classification

**Partially Supported / Qualified**

with:

> **Different Unit Granularity and Structural Responsibility**

as the primary structural observation.

# 3. Dependency Structure

## Projection A — World Model

The stable dependency relations include:

```text
Representation
      ↓
Understanding
```

and:

```text
Representation
      +
Understanding
      ↓
Prediction
```

Understanding therefore depends on Representation.

Prediction depends on both Representation and Understanding.

These are structural dependencies within the represented core.

They are not automatically execution steps or temporal sequences.

---

## Projection B — ACIM

The principal vertical dependency structure is represented as:

```text
Foundational Architecture
        ↓
Structural Generation
        ↓
Development Architecture
        ↓
Recovery Architecture
        ↓
Operational Architecture
```

Projection B additionally distinguishes:

- Hierarchical Dependency
- Functional Dependency
- Organizational Dependency

Cross-layer participation is possible without dissolving the primary vertical organization.

---

## Direct Structural Observation

At a high level, both representations contain a pattern in which downstream responsibilities depend on prior structural conditions.

This permits a limited abstraction:

```text
Prior Structural Condition
        ↓
Constrains / Enables
        ↓
Downstream Structural Responsibility
```

However, the dependency responsibilities differ materially.

Projection A primarily represents dependencies among representational and cognitive-functional responsibilities.

Projection B represents developmental and organizational dependencies across a layered architecture.

Reverse-direction examination also weakens any stronger correspondence.

Projection B's dependency from developed differentiation into Recovery has no equivalent dependency in Projection A.

Likewise, Projection A's Representation / Understanding dependency supporting Prediction has no equivalent dependency structure in Projection B.

### Restabilized Classification

**Limited Abstract Dependency-Organization Correspondence**

together with:

> **Different Dependency Responsibility**

No shared dependency mechanism is established.

```text

  ↓

  ↓

  ↓
Prediction
```

In particular:

Projection B assigns explicit architectural responsibility to Generation and Differentiation.

Its structure includes:

```text

    ↓
Generation
    ↓
Initial Differentiation
    ↓
Development
    ↓
Progressive Differentiation
```

Generation therefore occupies an explicit position in the represented architecture.

---

## Direct Structural Observation

The initial comparison allowed a partial correspondence at a highly abstract generative level.

Bounded Verification showed that this reading overstated Projection A.

Projection A supports relations and dependencies leading toward Understanding and Prediction, but it does not establish a generative architecture equivalent in responsibility to Projection B.

Projection B, by contrast, explicitly represents Generation and progressive Differentiation as architectural responsibilities.

### Restabilized Classification

**Material Non-Correspondence in Explicit Generative Architecture**

This does not establish that generation is absent from every possible interpretation of the World Model.

It establishes only that:

> **Frozen Projection A does not support an explicit generative architecture corresponding to Frozen Projection B.**

# Overall Structural Judgment

The two Frozen Representations exhibit meaningful correspondence at a formal representational level.

Both preserve:

- differentiated structural units;
- typed relations;
- dependency distinctions;
- responsibility boundaries;
- non-collapse invariants.

Limited correspondence also exists at an abstract organizational level where prior structural conditions constrain downstream responsibilities.

However, these correspondences do not extend into structural identity.

At the object-structural level, material differences remain stable.

Projection A is primarily:

> **Representation-centered, Prediction-directed, compact, and architecturally open beyond its stable core.**

Projection B is primarily:

> **Layer-anchored, developmentally organized, Recovery / Reintegration-oriented, operationally maintained, multi-dimensional, and explicitly closed as a Structural Representation.**

Therefore:

> **World Model and ACIM are not supported as structurally identical or topologically equivalent architectures.**

The strongest formal similarities remain meaningful but must be bounded by an unresolved provenance question:

> Do they reflect the research objects themselves, or the Structural Representation process through which the objects were reconstructed?

Within the current Frozen Representations, that question remains:

> **UNDETERMINED**
