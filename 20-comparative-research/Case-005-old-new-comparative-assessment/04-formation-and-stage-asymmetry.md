## 6. Stage Asymmetry

Formation Asymmetry produces **Stage Asymmetry**.

Stage Asymmetry occurs when one Case reaches an analytical responsibility that the other Case does not reach.

Suppose New contains observation X while Old contains no corresponding observation.

Several explanations remain possible:

    New Observation X
            ↓
    No corresponding Old judgment

may mean:

    A. Old formed a materially different judgment.

or:

    B. Old formed a broader or less differentiated judgment.

or:

    C. Old never reached the analytical stage responsible for forming X.

These conditions are not equivalent.

Only A or B permits ordinary direct comparison.

Condition C requires preservation of Stage Asymmetry.

# Part 2 — Stage-Unavailable and Direct Comparison Boundary

## 35. Definition of Stage-Unavailable

Within this Old × New comparative assessment:

> **STAGE-UNAVAILABLE** means that a New observation was formed within an analytical responsibility for which no materially comparable Legacy stage was formed.

This category does not mean that the relevant phenomenon was absent from the underlying research objects.

It does not mean that Old Case 005 rejected the observation.

It does not mean that Old Case 005 failed to identify the observation.

It means only:

> **The historical Legacy formation does not provide a formed judgment at the corresponding analytical responsibility.**

---

## 36. Stage-Unavailable ≠ New-Only

This distinction is essential.

**NEW-ONLY** would require evidence that:

1. New explicitly formed observation X;
2. Old had a materially comparable analytical opportunity to form X;
3. the relevant Old evidence does not contain X;
4. the difference cannot reasonably be explained by stage asymmetry.

By contrast:

**STAGE-UNAVAILABLE** means:

    New formed X
            ↓
    Old never reached
    the analytical responsibility
    responsible for X
            ↓
    No symmetric comparison available

Therefore:

> **Stage-Unavailable ≠ New-only**

---

    New formed observation X

    Old did not form X
            ↓
    Old failed to observe X

If the Legacy process never reached the corresponding analytical stage, no such performance judgment is justified.

The correct formulation is:

> **No formed Legacy judgment exists at the corresponding analytical responsibility.**

Therefore:

> **Stage-Unavailable ≠ Legacy Failure**

> **Stage-Unavailable ≠ Non-Reproduction**
