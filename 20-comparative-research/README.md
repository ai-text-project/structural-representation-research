# Comparative Research

**Domain:** `20-comparative-research/`  
**Repository:** `structural-representation-research`  
**Status:** Active Research Domain  
**Primary Responsibility:** Structural comparison among independently constructed Structural Representations

---

# Purpose

This domain contains comparative research conducted between Structural Representation cases.

The purpose of Comparative Research is not to compare raw source materials directly.

Instead, comparison begins after individual research objects have been structurally represented with sufficient independence and stability.

The central comparative principle is:

> **Compare Representations, not Resources.**

This preserves a clear responsibility boundary between:

```text
Research Object
      ↓
Structural Representation
      ↓
Comparative Research
```

Representation establishes the structure of an individual research object.

Comparative Research examines what becomes observable when independently constructed representations are placed into structural relation.

# Core Comparative Principle

The repository adopts the following discipline:

> **Compare Representations, not Resources.**

Research objects may differ substantially in:

- terminology,
- source format,
- conceptual vocabulary,
- disciplinary context,
- theoretical assumptions,
- explanatory conventions,
- and levels of abstraction.

Direct comparison of raw resources may therefore overemphasize surface similarity or terminology.

Structural Representation provides an intermediate research interface:

```text
Research Object A
        ↓
Structural Representation A
              │
              │
              ▼
       COMPARATIVE RESEARCH
              ▲
              │
              │
Structural Representation B
        ↑
Research Object B
```

Comparison therefore operates primarily on externalized structures.

Raw resources remain available as evidence and validation references, but they are not the primary comparative surface.

---

# Representation Independence

Comparative Research should preserve the independence of each Structural Representation.

The process should therefore follow:

```text
Object A
   ↓
Representation A
   │
   ├───────────────────┐
   │                   │
   │              Comparison
   │                   │
   └───────────────────┤
                       │
Representation B ──────┘
   ↑
Object B
```

and not:

```text
Desired Comparison
        ↓
Representation A modified
Representation B modified
        ↓
Artificial Correspondence
```

The comparative objective should not retroactively determine the structure of the representations being compared.

This principle protects representation integrity.

# Structural Correspondence

Structural Correspondence examines whether elements or relational patterns in independently constructed representations occupy structurally comparable positions.

The purpose is not to establish conceptual identity.

Instead, the question is approximately:

> **Do independently represented structures exhibit relationships that can be meaningfully placed into correspondence?**

Conceptually:

```text
Representation A              Representation B

   Structure A1  ◄────────►      Structure B1
       │                            │
       ▼                            ▼
   Structure A2  ◄────────►      Structure B2
```

Correspondence may be:

- strong,
- partial,
- conditional,
- asymmetric,
- absent,
- or structurally misleading.

Non-correspondence is therefore a valid research result.

---

# Functional Correspondence

Functional Correspondence examines whether structurally different components or relationships perform comparable roles within their respective systems.

This distinction is important because:

```text
Structural Similarity
        ≠
Functional Similarity
```

and:

```text
Structural Difference
        ≠
Functional Difference
```

Two components may differ structurally while participating in partially comparable functions.

Conversely, visually similar structures may perform fundamentally different functions.

Functional Correspondence should therefore be examined independently from surface structural resemblance.

---

# Hierarchical Projection

Hierarchical Projection examines relative structural positioning where hierarchy or level differentiation is supported by the representations.

Its purpose is not to impose a common hierarchy on heterogeneous research objects.

Instead, it asks whether relative organizational relationships can be projected into a comparative observation space.

For example:

```text
Representation A           Representation B

Higher Structure           Higher Structure
      │                           │
      ▼                           ▼
Intermediate                 Intermediate
      │                           │
      ▼                           ▼
Local Structure              Local Structure
```

Such projection is only meaningful when supported by the underlying representations.

Apparent vertical similarity should not be treated automatically as structural equivalence.

---

# Observation

Comparative observation records relationships that become visible only after representations are placed into relation.

These observations may include:

- correspondence,
- non-correspondence,
- asymmetry,
- structural inversion,
- functional divergence,
- different dependency patterns,
- different abstraction structures,
- different topology,
- and previously unrecognized relationships.

The purpose is not to maximize similarity.

The purpose is to increase structural observability.

---

# Comparative Summary

The Comparative Summary provides a stabilized account of the principal findings from an individual comparative case.

It should preserve:

- major correspondences,
- major differences,
- unresolved relationships,
- limitations,
- and evidence boundaries.

The summary should not convert partial correspondence into theoretical equivalence.

It functions as an interface between an individual comparative case and possible later Cross-Case Synthesis.

---

# Correspondence Is Not Equivalence

A fundamental boundary of this domain is:

> **Structural Correspondence does not imply theoretical equivalence.**

This can be expressed as:

```text
Correspondence
      ≠
Identity
      ≠
Equivalence
      ≠
Shared Theory
```

Two research objects may exhibit meaningful structural correspondence while differing substantially in:

- theoretical foundations,
- ontology,
- terminology,
- purpose,
- mechanisms,
- scale,
- or explanatory interpretation.

Comparative Research should preserve those differences.

---

# Difference as Research Evidence

Difference is not treated as failure of comparison.

Instead:

```text
Representation A
        │
        ▼
    Comparison
        ▲
        │
Representation B
        │
        ▼
 ┌───────────────┐
 │ Correspondence│
 │ Difference    │
 │ Asymmetry     │
 │ Non-alignment │
 └───────────────┘
        │
        ▼
Research Observation
```

A strong comparative framework should make both similarity and difference more legible.

The objective is therefore not convergence.

It is structural understanding.

# Comparison and Validation

Comparative findings should remain traceable to the underlying representations.

When a comparative relationship appears uncertain, the appropriate response is not necessarily to modify the representations.

Instead:

```text
Comparative Observation
        ↓
Uncertain Correspondence
        ↓
Return to Representation
        ↓
Check Structural Evidence
        ↓
Validate Interpretation
        ↓
Resume Comparison
```

This creates a feedback relationship between Representation and Comparison while preserving their separate responsibilities.

The feedback process should be used for validation rather than for forcing correspondence.

# Comparative Research Boundary

This domain distinguishes comparison from several adjacent activities.

```text
Structural Representation
          ≠
Comparative Research
          ≠
Cross-Case Synthesis
          ≠
Research Output
```

### Structural Representation

Asks:

> What is the structure of this research object?

### Comparative Research

Asks:

> What becomes observable when two or more Structural Representations are placed into relation?

### Cross-Case Synthesis

Asks:

> What recurring or higher-order observations become visible across multiple comparative cases?

### Research Output

Asks:

> How should stabilized research findings be externalized for a broader research or public context?

These responsibilities should remain distinguishable.

---

# Relationship to Cross-Case Synthesis

An individual comparative case should not automatically produce generalized conclusions.

The intended progression is:

```text
Representation A ─┐
                  ├─► Comparative Case 1
Representation B ─┘

Representation B ─┐
                  ├─► Comparative Case 2
Representation C ─┘

Representation A ─┐
                  ├─► Comparative Case 3
Representation C ─┘

                       │
                       ▼
              Multiple Comparative Cases
                       │
                       ▼
               Cross-Case Synthesis
```

`30-cross-case-synthesis/` should become increasingly important only after sufficient comparative evidence accumulates.

This preserves a distinction between:

```text
Observation within one comparison
               ≠
Pattern across comparisons
```

# Current Research Interpretation

At the present stage, `20-comparative-research/` can be understood as:

> **a research domain in which independently constructed Structural Representations are used as interfaces for examining structural correspondence, functional correspondence, difference, asymmetry, and other relationships across heterogeneous research objects.**

The objective is not to demonstrate that different research objects are fundamentally the same.

The objective is to make their relationships structurally observable.

# Boundary Conditions

This domain does **not** assume that:

- every Structural Representation should be compared,
- every pair of research objects has meaningful correspondence,
- correspondence implies equivalence,
- structural similarity implies theoretical similarity,
- functional similarity implies structural identity,
- hierarchy exists in every research object,
- all comparative cases require identical analytical dimensions,
- one comparative case is sufficient for cross-case generalization,
- or comparative findings should modify the underlying representations.

Comparative Research should remain evidence-driven and preserve structural difference.
